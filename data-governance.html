Know your data

Discover first. Don’t assume every bank has the same systems.

DPDP Rules require banks to identify personal data, its purposes and lawful basis, retention, sharing, cross-border transfers (if any), and security safeguards. Start with a practical data inventory that maps what data you hold, where it lives, who uses it, why it is used, and who may receive it.

Consent and notices

Tell people clearly. Respect their choice.

A notice is needed when people need to be told how their personal data is being handled. Consent may be required in some situations, but it is not the only lawful basis.

A clear notice should tell people

01

What data is involved

The types of personal data collected or used.

02

The purpose

Why it is collected and how it will be used.

03

How to exercise rights

How to access, correct, or withdraw consent (where applicable).

04

Contact / grievance path

Who to contact and how concerns will be resolved.

Operational steps for banks

01

Draft a plain-language notice

Write it so a customer can understand it quickly.

02

Identify where consent is required

Check each use of data against the applicable requirement.

03

Record the person’s choice clearly

Capture what was chosen, and when.

04

Make withdrawal handling clear and easy

Withdrawing should be as simple as giving consent.

05

Route the request to the right team

Send it to the owner without delay.

06

Keep evidence of notice and choices

Retain records you can produce later.

The customer’s journey

Step through what a customer experiences.

01

I am asked for my data

Personal data is requested for a purpose.

02

I see a clear notice

A notice in simple language about my data and rights.

03

I understand

I know what is happening to my data.

04

I choose where required

I give consent only where it is needed.

05

My choice is recorded

My choice and the time are recorded.

06

I can withdraw or raise a concern

I can change my choice or raise a grievance easily.

07

The bank responds

The bank acts on my request and closes the loop.

Customer request workflow

A simple, consistent six-step workflow.

A simple, consistent six-step workflow to manage customer requests under the DPDP Act and Rules.

01

Receive

Capture the request through approved channels and log it.

02

Acknowledge

Acknowledge receipt of the request to the customer.

03

Verify identity proportionately

Verify the requester’s identity using proportional measures.

04

Route to the owner

Route the request to the data fiduciary or data processor that owns the data.

05

Assess the applicable right and legal exceptions

Assess the request against the applicable right and any legal exemptions.

06

Respond and record

Respond to the customer and record the action taken.

Keep the trail

Keep clear, retrievable evidence for every request.

01

Request received

Date, channel, and request reference.

02

Identity check used

Method used and why it was proportional.

03

Owner assigned

Data fiduciary or processor and owner details.

04

Decision and reason

Right assessed, exceptions (if any), and decision.

05

Response sent

How and when the response was sent.

06

Closure record

Completion date, records retained, and reference ID.

See the phone-number correction example for this workflow in action. Legal applicability and dates must be checked against current official notifications and qualified legal advice.
