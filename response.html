Grievance handling

Accessible, accountable and evidence-based

We provide a clear channel for individuals to raise concerns about how their personal data is handled. Every grievance is tracked, assigned to a responsible case owner, addressed within the stated timeline in the source report, and closed with documented evidence.

SOURCE REPORT STATEMENTThe DPDP Rules describe a 90-day timeline for grievance redressal.
IMPORTANT CAVEATVerify the current applicable requirement and interpretation from official notifications and qualified legal advice before relying on this timeline.

Step through the workflow from receipt to closure.

Customer-friendly communication

Breach response playbook

Detect, contain, assess, notify as required.

Definition

Suspected incident

An event or behaviour that may indicate unauthorized access, loss, alteration or exposure of personal data.

Definition

Confirmed personal data breach

It is reasonably believed that personal data has been accessed, disclosed, altered, lost or destroyed in an unlawful or unauthorised manner.

Step-by-step playbook

1

Detect

Identify and validate signs of a possible incident through monitoring, reports or alerts.

2

Contact

Take immediate steps to prevent further unauthorised access or damage. Isolate systems or data carefully.

3

Preserve evidence

Preserve logs, system images and relevant records securely to support assessment and investigation.

4

Assess affected data and people

Assess the nature of personal data involved and the categories and number of individuals who may be affected.

5

Escalate

Inform the designated decision owner and assemble the response team. Keep leadership informed.

6

Notify as legally required

Notify the Data Protection Board and affected individuals as required under the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.

7

Recover

Restore systems and data safely. Validate normal operations before resuming services.

8

Learn

Document what happened, root causes and actions. Update controls and playbooks to reduce risk.

SOURCE REPORT STATEMENTAs per the EY India DPDP Rules report (Jan 2026), the Rules describe the requirement to notify “without undue delay” and to submit a detailed report to the Data Protection Board within 72 hours.
IMPORTANT CAVEATThe applicable rule, timing and interpretation must be checked against current official notifications and qualified legal advice before action.

Retention and safe deletion

Keep only what is needed, no longer than needed.

Responsible data retention supports purpose limitation, storage limitation and data fiduciary accountability. A purpose-based retention approach runs from collection to secure deletion, including backups, vendor copies and deletion evidence.

Purpose-based retention schedule (concept)

01

Data type

What personal data is collected or generated.

02

Purpose

Specific purpose(s) for which the data is processed.

03

Owner

Business or operational person responsible.

04

Review trigger

Event to review retention (e.g. end of purpose, account closure, inactivity).

05

Retention rule to confirm

Time period or condition to keep data for the stated purpose or applicable duty.

06

Deletion / anonymisation action

Secure deletion or anonymisation once purpose ends and no duty requires retention.

Practical flow: from collection to safe deletion
